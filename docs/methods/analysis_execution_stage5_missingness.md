# Analysis execution, stage 5: missingness audit

**Study:** Baseline CRP and all-cause mortality, NHANES 1999–2010, mortality follow-up through 2019
**Scope:** Pre-imputation descriptive QC for the multiple-imputation sensitivity
**Status:** Descriptive audit completed and reviewed; synthetic implementation test completed separately; real-data imputation not yet executed

## Objective and input cohorts

The frozen SAP specifies a sensitivity analysis with 50 multiply imputed datasets. Before selecting imputation models, Script 12 audits missingness in the mortality-linked primary eligible cohort generated by `04_build_analysis_cohort.R`. The audit is descriptive: it does not impute variables, fit regression models, or establish a missingness mechanism.

The intended imputation population is the primary eligible cohort after confirmed pregnancy exclusion, not merely the complete-case cohort. The primary imputation targets only missing values in the **primary adjustment covariates**. Family income-to-poverty ratio is audited separately because its inclusion belongs to a distinct additional-adjustment sensitivity.

Inputs:

- `data/processed/mortality_linked_eligible_cohort.rds`
- `data/processed/primary_complete_case_cohort.rds`
- frozen expected counts in `src/R/00_config.R`

## Integrity checks

Script 12 verifies expected eligible and complete-case counts, unique participant identifiers, agreement of the completeness indicator with the primary covariates, and participant-ID concordance between the eligible and complete-case cohorts. It also checks that CRP, event status, follow-up, weights, strata and PSUs have no missing values requiring imputation, and that weights, follow-up and event status pass basic validity conditions.

The reviewed summary outputs reconcile:

| Measure | Observed |
| --- | ---: |
| Primary eligible participants | 27,755 |
| Primary complete-case participants | 26,818 |
| Participants incomplete on at least one primary covariate | 937 (3.376%) |
| Complete on primary covariates and income-to-poverty ratio | 24,683 |
| Mortality events in primary eligible cohort | 6,354 |

The 468-event difference between the eligible cohort and the primary complete-case cohort is descriptive. It must not be assigned a causal interpretation or treated as a quantification of complete-case bias.

BMI accounts for the largest variable-specific missing count. Of the 937 participants with incomplete primary covariates, 550 have **only** BMI missing. The sum of missing values across variables is not the same as the number of incomplete participants, because participants can have more than one missing covariate.

## Missingness by mortality status

| Mortality status | Participants | Incomplete (n) | Incomplete (%) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Alive/censored | 21,401 | 469 | 2.191 |
| Died | 6,354 | 468 | 7.365 |

The incomplete-case percentage is substantially higher among those who died. Include mortality status and an appropriate representation of cumulative baseline hazard in the imputation model, as required by the frozen SAP. These descriptive differences do **not** establish that data are Missing At Random (MAR) or determine the direction or magnitude of any bias.

The 2001–2002 cycle has 235 of the 575 missing BMI observations (235/4,448 = 5.28% of that cycle). Other BMI missing counts by cycle are 46, 86, 71, 82 and 55 in chronological order. Hypertension missingness is concentrated more heavily in the first three cycles (44, 44 and 48), whereas factor-level stability still requires evaluation within the actual imputation models. No observed overall category count by itself establishes the absence of separation.

These are descriptive findings and were not used to inspect or optimize the CRP–mortality regression coefficient.

## Conclusion and next gate

The descriptive audit is complete and supports proceeding to imputation-model development. Six primary covariates require possible imputation; fixed exposure, outcome, follow-up, weights and survey identifiers must not be imputed. No imputation model is approved by this audit alone. The separate small synthetic software test has now completed successfully; see `docs/methods/analysis_execution_stage5_synthetic_validation.md`. Its success does not resolve the open real-data imputation-design choices. Finalize those choices and assess further diagnostics before imputing actual NHANES records.
